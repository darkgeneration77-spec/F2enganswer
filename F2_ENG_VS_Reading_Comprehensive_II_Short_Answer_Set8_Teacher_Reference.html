Set 8 — Being Different

Original Passage / 原文

Being Different I am left-handed. This means I am good at using my left hand to do things instead of my right hand. My parents tried teaching me how to use my right hand to do most things when I was growing up as they were worried that I would have a tough time in school. When I started going to school, I was told to write using my right hand only. I remember my teachers said, “That's what everyone does.” I struggled so much with using my right hand to write that my handwriting was always illegible. When the teachers could not read my writing, I was punished to write more so that I could improve. It was really an awful experience. In school, I did not have many friends. I was known as the stupid boy who could not write properly. I was punished so often that I did not have much time to study. This contributed to the reason why I did not do well in my examinations. This situation went on until Miss Audrey came to teach in my school. Miss Audrey assured me it was all right to use my left hand to write and do things. She even bought me a special pair of scissors that enables me to cut things with my left hand. Miss Audrey kept telling me to be proud of myself and that being different was what made me special. She also warned the other students to stop calling me stupid. Her kindness gave me the confidence and motivation to catch up with my studies. Today, I am a successful paediatrician in a hospital. I encourage children who come under my care while treating them of their illnesses. I always remember to tell them that it is all right if they are different. This is because being different is what makes them special.
